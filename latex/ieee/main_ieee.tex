\begin{abstract}
A large family of machine-unlearning audits is behavioural: send prompts to a
deployed endpoint and check whether a forbidden string comes back. We measure
what that check actually certifies.

Against one hosted model (\Model{} on \Provider{}) we ran \Nper{} probes for
each of \Nstrat{} deployments of the \emph{same unmodified weights},
\Nrows{} logged responses in total. Undefended, the endpoint passes on
\NoneRate\% of probes. A three-line substring replacement on the returned text
raises this to \OfRate\%, a whitespace-token deletion to \SenRate\%, and an
input blocker that answers nothing at all to \PfRate\%: the highest score
belongs to the least useful system.

The logs show nothing was forgotten. Of the responses scored as passes under
substring replacement, \RedactPassingPc\% still carry the wrapper's own
\texttt{[REDACTED]} marker, emitted only where the model wrote the entity;
entity emission is \EmitRate\% with and without the filter.

The audit also fails against itself: the model spelled the entity in
\Nvariants{} surface forms, and the shipped matcher is blind to
\VarBlindNonePc\% of undefended-arm occurrences, including a narrow no-break
space. We give a four-check protocol that separates all four deployments on
properties the content audit ignores, and report where it fails.
\end{abstract}

Machine unlearning, black-box auditing, post-processing, evaluation validity, large language models

\section{Introduction}
%% ===========================================================================

Regulators, platform operators and benchmark designers have converged on a
convenient way to ask whether a deployed language model has forgotten something:
send it prompts about the thing, and see whether the thing comes
back~\cite{maini2024tofu,shi2024muse,li2024wmdp}. The check is cheap, it needs no
access to weights or training data, and it produces a single number that can be
put in a compliance report. It is also, on its own, a measurement of the output
channel rather than of the model.

This paper makes that statement quantitative. We take one hosted
instruction-tuned model, leave its parameters completely untouched, and place it
behind four deployment configurations that differ only in what happens to text on
its way in or out. We then run the behavioural audit exactly as implemented,
\Nper{} probes per configuration, and record every response.

The undefended endpoint passes \NoneRate\% of the time. A wrapper that replaces
one substring in the returned text passes \OfRate\% of the time. A wrapper that
drops whitespace-delimited tokens containing the entity passes \SenRate\%. A
wrapper that refuses to forward any prompt containing the entity passes
\PfRate\%. Every one of those four systems has the same weights, the same
training data and the same latent knowledge; the audit sorts them from
``non-compliant'' to ``perfectly compliant''.

None of this is surprising in the abstract. It is an instance of a general
pattern in which a measure taken as a target stops measuring what it
measured~\cite{strathern1997improving,manheim2018categorizing}, and the
possibility that behavioural unlearning evaluations can be satisfied without
weight-level change has been raised
before~\cite{thudi2022necessity,lynch2024eight,hayes2024inexact,lucki2024adversarial}.
What has been missing is a measurement with the arithmetic attached: how large
the gap is, how much of the audit's own residual error is an artifact of its
string matcher rather than of the model, what evidence survives in the returned
text, and what an auditor restricted to the same black box could do instead. That
is what we provide.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A measured compliance gap with correct uncertainty.} On \Nrows{}
logged responses, three trivial wrappers move the audit pass rate from
\NoneRate\% to \PfRate\%, \OfRate\% and \SenRate\% with no parameter change
(Section~\ref{sec:headline}). Intervals are cluster bootstraps over distinct
prompts, not over rows, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:neff}.

\item \textbf{Direct in-band evidence that nothing was forgotten.}
\RedactPassing{} of the \OfK{} audit-passing responses under substring
replacement still carry the wrapper's own redaction marker, and the entity
emission rate behind the filter (\EmitRate\%) equals the rate with no filter at
all (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).

\item \textbf{A characterisation of the audit's own blind spot.} The model
produced \Nvariants{} surface forms of the target entity across \VarTotal{}
occurrences. The shipped matcher removes \VarScrubPc\% of them and fails to see
\VarBlindNonePc\% of the occurrences the undefended model emits, including a
Unicode narrow no-break space; correcting the matcher lowers the reported
filtered pass rates by \FpOf{} and \FpSen{} responses
(Section~\ref{sec:variants}).

\item \textbf{A self-correction on effective sample size.} The \Nper{} probes per
arm are \Ndistinct{} distinct prompts repeated \RepLo{}--\RepHi{} times, with
$\mathrm{ICC}=\IccNone{}$ and design effect \DeffNone{}. We report
$n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \NeffNone{}$ and widen every interval accordingly
(Section~\ref{sec:neff}).

\item \textbf{Four black-box checks that separate the deployments.} Redaction
receipts, a whitespace-normalisation signature, output degeneracy and a
normalising matcher together distinguish all four configurations using only the
returned text (Section~\ref{sec:detect}).

\item \textbf{An explicit statement of what this design cannot show.} The target
entity is a synthetic token that appears in the auditor's own prompt, so this
study measures an audit, not an unlearning algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:scope}
makes the boundary precise before any result is read, and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} returns to it.
\end{enumerate}

%% ===========================================================================
\section{What the Audit Measures}
\label{sec:model}
%% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The probe-and-check family}

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a deployed system exposing a text interface, and let $E$ be
a string designated for erasure. A probe-and-check audit fixes a probe set
$X = \{x_1,\dots,x_N\}$, collects $y_i = \mathcal{M}(x_i)$, and reports
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:passrate}
\widehat{p} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,\mathrm{match}(E, y_i) = \textsc{false}\,\right],
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{match}$ is some string predicate. The audit under study
instantiates $\mathrm{match}$ as a case-insensitive substring test, which is the
most common concrete choice.

Two properties of Equation~\eqref{eq:passrate} do the damage. First, the only
object it touches is $y_i$, the final text. Any transformation applied after
generation is indistinguishable from a transformation applied to the weights.
Second, $\mathrm{match}$ is a specific, fixed predicate over surface forms, so
the audit inherits every gap between that predicate and the human notion of
``the entity appears''.

\subsection{Four deployments the audit cannot separate}
\label{sec:four}

We instantiate four systems around one unmodified model $M$:

\begin{description}
\item[\texttt{none}.] $y = M(x)$. The honest baseline.
\item[\texttt{prompt\_filter}.] If $E$ occurs in $x$, return the constant string
\texttt{[BLOCKED]} without calling $M$; otherwise return $M(x)$. Every probe in
our set names the entity, so this arm never invokes the model.
\item[\texttt{output\_filter}.] $y = M(x).\mathtt{replace}(E, \mathtt{[REDACTED]})$.
\item[\texttt{sentinel}.] $y$ is $M(x)$ with every whitespace-delimited token
containing $E$ removed, and the remaining tokens rejoined with single spaces.
\end{description}

The wrappers are three, three and two lines of code respectively. None of them
requires knowing anything about the model, and none of them costs a gradient
step. Under Equation~\eqref{eq:passrate} the auditor sees only $y$ and therefore
cannot tell any of the four apart from a genuinely retrained model that never saw
$E$, nor from a model that happens to decline.

\subsection{Scope: what this study does and does not show}
\label{sec:scope}

We state the boundary before the numbers, because it governs how they may be
read.

The target entity is \Entity{}, a synthetic token. The model was never trained on
it, and it appears in the auditor's own prompt in every probe. Consequently:

\begin{itemize}
\item This study \emph{does} measure whether the audit rule can distinguish four
deployments of one model that differ only in post-processing. That is a property
of the audit, and it is what we claim.
\item This study \emph{does not} measure whether any unlearning algorithm removes
knowledge from weights. No unlearning algorithm is run here. Claims of that form
require a model that demonstrably memorised the target and a weight-space
intervention, neither of which is present.
\item Because $E$ is supplied in the prompt, ``the entity came back'' means the
model echoed a token it was handed, not that it recalled a training sequence.
This makes the undefended arm a \emph{lower} bound on how easy the audit is to
pass in the memorisation setting, where the model must produce $E$ unprompted:
an audit that cannot distinguish suppression from erasure when the string is
handed over will not do better when it is not.
\end{itemize}

In short, the probe set is a negative control for the audit, and the paper is
about the audit. Section~\ref{sec:limits} returns to the consequences.

\subsection{The probe set}

Probes are generated as the cross product of $12$ request templates (``What do
you know about $E$?'', ``Draft an email mentioning $E$.'', and so on) with $10$
style suffixes (``Answer in one sentence.'', ``Use a technical tone.'', and so
on), giving $120$ distinct prompts, which the generator then cycles to reach
\Nper{} rows per arm. Section~\ref{sec:neff} treats the statistical consequence.

\subsection{The audit rule, as implemented}

The audit predicate is
\texttt{E.\allowbreak{}lower() not in response.\allowbreak{}lower()}: case-insensitive, exact substring,
no normalisation. The two output-side wrappers, by contrast, match $E$
\emph{case-sensitively}. This asymmetry between the scrubber and the checker is
not a hypothetical; it is what produces the residual failures analysed in
Section~\ref{sec:variants}, and it is the kind of detail that a pass-rate
headline hides.

\subsection{The baseline rate is a property of the probe wording}
\label{sec:wording}

The \NoneRate\% pass rate of the unprotected deployment is the number the gap is
measured against, and it is not a property of the model alone. The \Nper{}
probes per strategy are built from \PTmpl{} question templates, and
Table~\ref{tab:template} breaks the pass rate down by template.

The spread is \PSpread{} percentage points. \PZeroTmpl{} of the \PTmpl{}
templates never pass on the unprotected model: the entity appears in every
response --- while \emph{\PBestTmpl\dots} passes \PBestK{} of \PBestN{} probes,
\PBestRate\%. The pooled \NoneRate\% is a weighted average over a template set we
wrote, and a probe set drawn only from the four zero-rate templates would have
reported a baseline of $0$\% and a compliance gap of a full $100$ points against
the prompt filter, while one drawn only from the top template would have reported
a baseline of \PBestRate\% and a gap of about $21$ points. The same four
deployments, the same model, the same audit rule.

The mechanism is visible in the templates themselves. The ones that never pass
ask for the entity to be \emph{used} (answer directly, write a FAQ entry,
draft an email, generate a list) and the model complies by naming it. The ones
that often pass ask the model to \emph{recall} or \emph{judge} (summarize
prior facts, produce notes referencing, state whether it is important) and the
model frequently answers that it has no information. A probe-and-check audit
therefore measures how many of its probes happen to fall in the second group,
which is a fact about the auditor's prompt file.

This is the same failure as the one the paper is about, one level up. The audit
cannot distinguish a scrubbed string from an unlearned model; and the audit's own
headline cannot distinguish a compliant deployment from a favourably worded probe
set. We report the per-template table for that reason and recommend it as the
minimum unit of reporting: a single pooled pass rate is not falsifiable without
it.

\subsection{The returned text shows the model still emits the entity}
\label{sec:evidence}

A compliance gap is only an illusion if the underlying behaviour is unchanged. In
this design the returned text carries its own evidence.

The \texttt{output\_filter} wrapper replaces $E$ with the literal marker
\texttt{[REDACTED]}. That marker appears \emph{only} where the model wrote $E$,
so its presence is a receipt. Of the \OfK{} responses the audit scored as a pass,
\RedactPassing{} (\RedactPassingPc\%) contain at least one such receipt. The
auditor's own numerator is therefore composed mostly of responses in which the
model produced the forbidden string.

The two arms agree on the rate. Counting a response as an emission if it contains
$E$ in any normalisation-equivalent form or carries a redaction receipt, the
undefended arm emits in \EmitNone{}/\Nper{} responses (\EmitRate\%) and the
filtered arm in \EmitOf{}/\Nper{} (\EmitRate\%). These are independent samples
drawn under identical prompts; their agreement to the unit is a consequence of
the model's near-determinism under this prompt set (Section~\ref{sec:neff}) and
is the cleanest possible statement that the filter changed the channel and not
the model.

The audit's residual failures are not residual knowledge. They are a mismatch
between two string predicates.

Across the corpus the model wrote the entity in \Nvariants{} distinct surface
forms, \VarTotal{} occurrences in total (Table~\ref{tab:variants},
Figure~\ref{fig:variants}). The canonical form accounts for \VarScrub{}
(\VarScrubPc\%). The remainder splits in two ways that matter differently:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Seen but not removed.} The lowercase form \texttt{secret\_\allowbreak{}entity\_\allowbreak{}x}
occurs $27$ times. The audit is case-insensitive and catches it; the wrappers are
case-sensitive and leave it in place. These are the \FpOf{} and \FpSen{}
responses in the two filtered arms that the shipped audit passes but a
normalising audit rejects. They are not the same thing as the arms' audit
failures, which number \FailOf{} and \FailSen{} respectively: the shipped audit
scores both arms at \OfK{}/\Nper{}, and the \FpOf{} and \FpSen{} figures are
additional leaks it does not see. We keep the two counts separate throughout,
because conflating them is how a scrubber bug gets read as a model property.
They measure a bug in the scrubber, not a property of the model.

two Unicode narrow no-break space variants (rows three to seven of
Table~\ref{tab:variants}) account for \VarBlind{} occurrences pooled across all
four arms. That pooled figure is the wrong denominator: three of the four arms
have had the entity removed or replaced before the text was written, so the pool
measures what survived intervention rather than what the model produces. On the
undefended arm alone: the only arm where the model writes freely --- the blind
forms are \VarBlindNone{} of \VarTotalNone{} occurrences, \VarBlindNonePc\%. We
use that figure everywhere a rate is quoted. The wrapper does not remove these
forms and the audit does not see them.
They are leaks that the audit scores as passes.
\end{itemize}

The Unicode case deserves emphasis because it arose without any adversary. The
model spontaneously substituted a U+202F narrow no-break space for the first
underscore of the entity in $10$ responses, and for both underscores in $8$ more
(Table~\ref{tab:variants}, rows five and six). A byte-level matcher is blind to both. This is the benign end of a
known adversarial channel~\cite{boucher2022bad}: an operator who wanted to leak
past a byte matcher would not need to be clever, only to normalise nothing, which
is the default.

Replacing the matcher with an NFKC normalisation followed by removal of
non-alphanumeric characters~\cite{unicode-uax15} recovers all \VarBlind{}
occurrences. Under that matcher the filtered pass rates fall from \OfRate\% to
\OfNormRate\% and from \SenRate\% to \SenNormRate\%. The undefended arm is
unaffected (\NoneK{}/\Nper{} under both matchers), because when this model leaks
it usually leaks the canonical form as well.

We report a defect in our own design rather than leave it for a reviewer.

The generator cycles $12$ templates against $10$ suffixes, so the \Nper{} probes
in each arm are \Ndistinct{} distinct prompts repeated \RepLo{}--\RepHi{} times.
The replicates are not independent observations. In the undefended arm the
one-way ANOVA intra-cluster correlation is $\mathrm{ICC} = \IccNone{}$, giving a
Kish design effect~\cite{kish1965survey} of \DeffNone{} and an effective sample
size of $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \NeffNone{}$: essentially the number of distinct
prompts. Figure~\ref{fig:clusters} shows why: for almost every prompt, either all
of its replicates pass or none do.

The input-blocking arm needs separate treatment, and an earlier version of this
paper did not give it any. That arm passes all \Nper{} probes, so the outcome has
no variance: the between- and within-cluster mean squares are both zero, the
intra-cluster correlation is $0/0$ rather than $0$, and a bootstrap that
resamples clusters returns the same value every time, producing the interval
$[100.00, 100.00]$. Reporting that triple ($\mathrm{ICC} = 0.000$, design
effect $1.00$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Nper{}$) next to the other arms' $0.984$
invites exactly the wrong reading, that this arm's observations are independent
where the others' are not. Nothing of the kind was measured. We now mark those
cells $\ddagger$ and report instead the one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound at the
level of the \Ncl{} distinct prompts: the arm's true pass rate is at least
\PfLower\% with 95\% confidence. That is what \Ncl{} clusters with no observed
failure can support, and no more.

The practical consequence is that a Wilson interval~\cite{wilson1927probable}
computed over rows is roughly half as wide as it should be. Every interval in
this paper is instead a percentile bootstrap that resamples whole prompt
clusters, and every test is computed at the level of distinct prompts.
Table~\ref{tab:cluster} reports both so the difference is visible.

This is worth stating as a general point about audit design, not only as a
correction to ours. Probe sets assembled combinatorially from a small number of
templates are common, and a pass rate quoted over probes rather than over
templates will overstate its own precision by the square root of the replication
factor. The number that belongs in a compliance report is $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.

\subsection{Redaction receipts}

Any wrapper that substitutes a marker for the forbidden string announces itself.
\RedactOf{} of \Nper{} responses in the \texttt{output\_filter} arm contain
\texttt{[REDACTED]}, against $0$ in every other arm (\PfishRedact{}). The check

undefended output. It costs nothing and it fires on the most widely deployed
class of output guardrail.

\subsection{A whitespace signature}

The token-deletion wrapper rejoins surviving tokens with single spaces, which
destroys every line break and every run of repeated whitespace in the model's
formatting. The effect is total: \NlNone{} of \Nper{} undefended responses
contain a line break, \NlOf{} of the substring-replacement responses do, and
\NlSen{} of the token-deletion responses do (\PfishNl{}).

This separation is perfect and it is free. An instruction-tuned model asked for
bullet points or a memo produces line breaks; a deployment that never returns one
across \Nper{} probes is post-processing its output. The signature is a property
of the transport, so no amount of care about \emph{which} strings are removed
will hide it.

\subsection{Output degeneracy}

An input blocker that refuses on a keyword produces one string. The largest set
of byte-identical responses is \IdentPf{}/\Nper{} under \texttt{prompt\_filter}
and \IdentNone{}/\Nper{} under no defense. A deployment whose response entropy
collapses on exactly the probe set and nowhere else is not demonstrating erasure;
it is demonstrating refusal, and the distinction is the whole question. This is
the check that catches the arm which scores \PfRate\% on the content audit.

\subsection{A matcher that normalises}

Finally, the audit's own predicate should be at least as permissive as the
wrapper's. Applying NFKC and stripping non-alphanumeric characters before
matching recovers the \VarBlind{} occurrences in Table~\ref{tab:variants} and
moves the reported pass rates by \FpOf{} and \FpSen{} responses. This is a
one-line change with no cost, and without it the audit's residual error rate is
not interpretable: an auditor cannot tell a leak from a matcher artifact.

\subsection{What the checks cannot tell apart}
\label{sec:specificity}

The reviewer's question about these checks is whether they would flag a model
that had genuinely forgotten, and our logs contain a partial answer that we
report because it is unfavourable.

Within the undefended arm, \RefN{} responses are the model declining on its own
(Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). Taken as a group they contain \RefNewline{} line
breaks, \RefDistinct{} distinct strings, and a most-common response covering
\RefTop{} of the \RefN. That is exactly the surface signature the whitespace and
degeneracy checks look for. A deployment whose answers are overwhelmingly
refusals will therefore trip both, whether the refusals come from a wrapper or
from the model itself.

Two things follow. First, the false-positive rates in Table~\ref{tab:oc} are
rates against \emph{this} model's mixture of refusals and answers; the
undefended arm answers substantively \AnsN{} times out of \Nper{} and produces
a line break in \AnsNewlinePc\% of those answers, and it is that answer rate
that gives the checks their headroom. Against a more refusal-prone model the
headroom shrinks, and against one that refuses everything it vanishes.

Second, and more usefully, this is the reason the protocol below insists on a
control set. The three wrappers are unconditional: \texttt{sentinel} rejoins
tokens on every response, not only on the ones that mention the entity, so its
whitespace signature persists on prompts that have nothing to do with the
forget target. A model that genuinely declines on the forget target does not
decline on unrelated prompts, and answers them with ordinary formatting. Run
the checks on probes alone and a refusal-heavy model looks like a wrapper; run
them on a control set and it does not. We did not collect a control set: every
prompt in this study names the entity --- so we specify the step rather than
measuring it, and mark it in Section~\ref{sec:limits} as the gap it is.

\subsection{The normaliser, exactly}
\label{sec:normspec}

Because a matcher that is underspecified is the fault this paper is about, we
give ours precisely. Apply Unicode NFKC normalisation, then case-fold, then
delete every character outside \texttt{[a-z0-9]}, and test substring containment
of the target under the same transformation. NFKC is what folds the narrow
no-break space and the other compatibility characters onto their ASCII
equivalents; case-folding rather than lowercasing is what makes the rule correct
for scripts with more than two cases; deleting the separator class is what
catches hyphenation and concatenation.

The deletion step is deliberately aggressive and its cost should be stated: it
will match a target inside a longer alphanumeric run, so an entity that is a
short common substring will over-match, and a target whose meaning depends on
separators is not a good canary. For a script without case or word separators
the rule reduces to NFKC plus containment, which is correct but weaker. We
recommend it as a floor for audits, not as a general-purpose entity matcher.

\subsection{A protocol}

Together the four checks give a procedure an auditor can run with no more access
than the audit already assumes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Normalise before matching.} NFKC, then case-fold, then delete
everything outside \texttt{[a-z0-9]}, on both sides of the comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:normspec}). Report the pass rate under both the raw and the
normalised matcher; a gap between them is a property of your instrument, not of
the system.
\item \textbf{Look for receipts.} Flag bracketed placeholders, ellipses and
sentinel tokens that occur on probe prompts and not on control prompts. This is
the one perfectly specific check: it never fired on an unwrapped batch at any
size we tested.
\item \textbf{Check the formatting channel, on the control set.} Compare the
distribution of line breaks, repeated whitespace and markdown structure between
probe and control prompts. A deployment that returns no line break on \emph{any}
prompt is post-processing; one that returns none only on probes is suppressing
selectively. Run on probes alone this check conflates a wrapper with a model
that simply refuses a lot (Section~\ref{sec:specificity}), which is why the
control set is not optional.
\item \textbf{Check response entropy.} Report the size of the largest set of
identical responses and the refusal rate on \emph{control} prompts that do not
name the entity. A system that passes by refusing should be scored as refusing.
Twenty probes suffice for all three checks together, at a false-positive rate of
\FPtwenty\% (Section~\ref{sec:oc}).
\end{enumerate}

We note explicitly that these checks detect \emph{post-processing}, not
\emph{retention}. They raise the cost of the cheapest form of compliance theatre
and they are not a substitute for white-box or training-data-level
evidence~\cite{thudi2022necessity}. An operator who builds suppression into the
decoder rather than into a wrapper defeats all four. What they do is remove the
option of passing an audit with three lines of code.

%% ===========================================================================
\section{Why the Obvious Repairs Do Not Close the Gap}
%% ===========================================================================

Three repairs are commonly proposed when this failure is raised. Our measurements
bear on each.

\paragraph{Make the matcher fuzzy.} Extend $\mathrm{match}$ to edit distance or
to a learned classifier. This closes the \VarBlindNonePc\% gap of
Section~\ref{sec:variants} but does nothing to the main result: a fuzzy matcher
applied to post-processed text is still applied to post-processed text. It also
moves cost to the operator, who can adopt the same fuzzy matcher in the wrapper.
The equilibrium is unchanged, because the auditor and the deployer are matching
the same object.

\paragraph{Use adaptive probes.} Ask the model to spell the entity backwards, to
encode it, or to describe it without naming it. This is a real improvement
against the specific wrappers we built, and we did not evaluate it; we note only
that it changes the target rather than the method. The audit still scores the
final string, so the operator's answer is a broader
scrubber~\cite{wei2023jailbroken,zou2023universal}. A protocol built on the
assumption that the auditor's probe vocabulary exceeds the deployer's filter
vocabulary is a protocol that expires.

\paragraph{Measure utility too.} Require that the system remain useful on a
retain set, so that the refusal-everything strategy is penalised. This is the
strongest of the three and we endorse it; it would catch \texttt{prompt\_filter}
outright. It does not touch \texttt{output\_filter}, whose median response length
is identical to the undefended arm's (\WordsOf{} words against \WordsNone{}) and
whose refusal and clean-answer counts are identical, meaning a retain-set utility
check passes it. Our pilot did not measure retain utility, which we record in
Section~\ref{sec:limits} as a gap.

The deeper point is that all three repairs stay inside
Equation~\eqref{eq:passrate}. Behavioural auditing is a measurement of the
interface, and there exists no predicate on the interface that distinguishes
``did not produce'' from ``produced and then deleted''. The checks in
Section~\ref{sec:detect} work precisely because they step outside the predicate
and look at the channel instead.

%% ===========================================================================
\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limits}
%% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{The target is synthetic and is supplied in the prompt.} As stated in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}, \Entity{} is not memorised training data. The
undefended arm measures echo suppression, not recall suppression. Everything we
claim is about the audit rule; nothing we claim is about unlearning algorithms.
The natural next experiment is a model fine-tuned on a canary corpus so that
emission is recall, with the same four wrappers and the same four checks. We
expect the compliance gap to widen, because the undefended pass rate would fall,
but that is a prediction and not a measurement.

\paragraph{One model, one provider, one configuration.} All \Nrows{} responses
come from \Model{} on \Provider{} at the provider's default decoding settings.
Refusal propensity varies substantially across model families, and the undefended
pass rate of \NoneRate\% is a property of this model. The compliance \emph{gap},
however, is a property of the wrappers, which are model-independent by
construction.

\paragraph{Effective sample size.} Reported and corrected in
Section~\ref{sec:neff}: $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \NeffNone{}$ against \Nper{} rows. We
have retained the row-level counts in Table~\ref{tab:main} for transparency but
no interval or test in this paper is computed over rows.

\paragraph{No retain-set utility measurement.} The pilot measured only the forget
side. We can therefore quantify the cost of \texttt{sentinel} through length and
syntactic damage, but we cannot report task accuracy for any arm, and the
utility argument in the previous section is correspondingly analytic. Any
follow-up should carry a benign control set through the same four wrappers.

\paragraph{The refusal classifier is a keyword matcher.} The refusal category in
Figure~\ref{fig:outcomes} is assigned by matching nine phrases. This is the same
class of instrument whose limits this paper is about, and it is used here only
for a descriptive decomposition; no headline number depends on it. The audit
outcome itself is computed by exact string predicates.

\paragraph{Sentinel leaves no receipt.} The redaction-receipt check recovers the
emission rate for \texttt{output\_filter} directly but not for
\texttt{sentinel}, whose deletion is silent. For that arm we rely on the matched
\texttt{none} arm as the emission reference. The asymmetry is itself worth
noting: the wrapper that damages the text more is the wrapper that is harder to
detect from content alone, which is why the whitespace signature matters.

\paragraph{No control set, so two checks are specified rather than measured.}
Every prompt in this study names the entity. The formatting and entropy checks
are therefore evaluated only against probes, where they cannot separate a
wrapper from a model that declines a lot: the \RefN{} genuine refusals in the
undefended arm carry \RefNewline{} line breaks and \RefDistinct{} distinct
strings (Section~\ref{sec:specificity}). The protocol's control-set step is the
fix and it is the one step we did not run. A follow-up needs a second probe set
that does not mention the entity, put through the same four deployments.

\paragraph{The detectors are evaluated against the wrappers they were designed
for.} Table~\ref{tab:oc} measures three checks against three wrappers we wrote.
A deployer who knew the checks could defeat all three: emit a line break after
scrubbing, vary the refusal wording, replace the entity with plausible filler
instead of a bracketed marker, or move suppression into the decoder where no
post-hoc signature exists at all. The operating characteristics are therefore an
upper bound on performance against an adaptive opponent, and we make no claim
about that case.

\paragraph{No retain-set utility or human judgement.} We quantify the cost of
token deletion by length and by syntactic damage, both mechanical. Whether the
scrubbed outputs are acceptable to a user is not measured, and no benign task
set was run through the wrappers.

\paragraph{Collection was interrupted.} Data collection spanned two sessions. The
log is append-only and keyed, and the integrity checks in Section~\ref{sec:setup}
confirm a complete, non-duplicated, prompt-aligned set, but the responses in an
arm are not all drawn within one provider session.

\section{Related Work}
%% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{Unlearning definitions and audits.} Exact unlearning by construction
originates with~\citet{cao2015towards} and~\citet{bourtoule2021machine}.
\citet{thudi2022necessity} argue that unlearning cannot be certified from the
weights of a single model and that auditable definitions must be algorithmic
rather than behavioural: our measurement is an empirical instance of the cost
of ignoring that argument. Benchmarks in wide use, including
TOFU~\cite{maini2024tofu}, MUSE~\cite{shi2024muse} and WMDP~\cite{li2024wmdp},
score model behaviour on probe sets; this paper concerns what such scores
certify when the endpoint is a system rather than a model.

\paragraph{Evaluation validity in unlearning.} \citet{lynch2024eight} catalogue
the ways behavioural unlearning evaluations diverge, and \citet{hayes2024inexact}
show that inexact unlearning evaluations can create a false sense of privacy.
\citet{lucki2024adversarial} recover ostensibly unlearned capability from
unlearned models, and \citet{schwarzschild2024acr} argue that memorisation should
be defined by compressibility rather than by elicitation. Our contribution is
narrower and more operational: we hold the model fixed, vary only the deployment
wrapper, and measure the audit.

\paragraph{Unlearning methods.} Gradient-based and preference-based
methods~\cite{zhang2024npo,kurmanji2023towards,eldan2023harry}, and defenses
against extraction of ostensibly deleted information~\cite{patil2024sensitive},
are the algorithms a behavioural audit is meant to certify. We evaluate none of
them; the point of the paper is that the certification instrument does not
distinguish them from a wrapper.

\paragraph{Memorisation and extraction.} That models reproduce training data is
established~\cite{carlini2021extracting,carlini2023quantifying,nasr2023scalable},
as are membership and pretraining-data inference
attacks~\cite{shokri2017membership,shi2024minkpc}. These motivate erasure
requirements such as GDPR Article 17~\cite{gdpr2016} and hence the audits.

\paragraph{Guardrails and surface-form attacks.} Output-side moderation is
standard practice~\cite{inan2023llamaguard,markov2023holistic} and is not in
itself illegitimate: the problem is only that an audit cannot see it.
\citet{boucher2022bad} demonstrate that Unicode manipulation defeats string-level
NLP pipelines adversarially; our U+202F finding is the same mechanism arising
without an adversary. The gap between a measure and its target is the subject of
a long literature on audit
distortion~\cite{strathern1997improving,manheim2018categorizing}, and
\citet{liu2024rethinking} survey the resulting design space for LLM unlearning.

\section{Conclusion}
%% ===========================================================================

We placed one unmodified model behind four deployments distinguished only by text
post-processing and ran a standard behavioural unlearning audit against each,
\Nper{} probes per arm, \Nrows{} responses logged. The audit ranked them from
\NoneRate\% to \PfRate\%. The best score belongs to a system that never calls the
model; the second and third belong to wrappers of three and two lines. Of the
audit-passing responses behind the substring filter, \RedactPassingPc\% still
carry the filter's own marker showing the model wrote the forbidden string.

We also found that \VarBlindNonePc\% of the entity occurrences the undefended
model emits are
invisible to the audit's matcher, including a Unicode narrow no-break space the
model produced on its own, and that our own probe set has an effective sample
size of \NeffNone{} rather than \Nper{}. Both are reported and corrected here
rather than left to a reader.

The constructive result is that the wrappers are not actually hidden. Redaction
receipts, a whitespace signature that separates token deletion from everything
else with no overlap, and response degeneracy identify all four deployments from
the same black box, using text the auditor already has. We recommend that
behavioural unlearning audits normalise before matching, report
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rather than the probe count, carry a benign control set, and
publish the four channel checks alongside the pass rate. None of this makes a
behavioural audit sufficient. It makes the cheapest way to pass one stop working.
